# Project overview

## JAL-AESR INDIA

**JAL-AESR INDIA** proposes a national framework for assessing agricultural water security by integrating the timing and behaviour of **rainfall, surface water and groundwater** with **crop-water demand, irrigation dependence and agro-ecological context**.

### Central scientific question

**How does the dominant agricultural water-source regime—groundwater buffering, direct rainfall dependence, or surface-water storage buffering—modify the translation of climate variability into crop-stage water availability across India’s contrasting agro-ecological systems?**

### Core proposition

Agricultural water security cannot be inferred from rainfall, groundwater or surface water independently. It emerges from their seasonal coupling with irrigation access and crop-water demand.

A general state representation is:

\[
\mathrm{AgroWaterState}_{i,t}=f(\mathrm{SurfaceWater}_{i,t},\mathrm{SoilMoisture}_{i,t},\mathrm{Groundwater}_{i,t},\mathrm{Recharge}_{i,t},\mathrm{IrrigationDependence}_{i,t},\mathrm{CropWaterDemand}_{i,t})
\]

where location \(i\) and time \(t\) are evaluated in a crop- and agro-ecologically explicit framework.

### Operational definition

**Irrigation security** is defined as the capacity of the accessible surface–groundwater system to meet crop-water requirements during critical growth stages with acceptable reliability and without persistent groundwater depletion.

## National objectives

1. Build a harmonized national surface-water–groundwater–irrigation data cube.
2. Quantify seasonal surface-water availability, persistence and reliability.
3. Characterize soil-moisture and irrigation response using NISAR, Indian Earth-observation missions and complementary sensors.
4. Quantify groundwater stress, recovery and recharge behaviour from monitored observations, with InSAR used only where physically appropriate.
5. Evaluate source coupling, irrigation dependence and crop-stage water sufficiency across agro-ecological regions.
6. Develop and independently validate a national agricultural water-resilience framework and Digital Water Observatory.

## Scientific novelty

The novelty is not the isolated use of NISAR, water mapping or machine learning. It is the explicit integration of remotely sensed surface response with monitored groundwater behaviour, surface-water dynamics, irrigation dependence, crop-water timing and agro-ecological context within one transferable national framework.

NISAR is treated as a high-frequency SAR observation capability for near-surface moisture, agricultural response, inundation and—where suitable—surface deformation. It is **not** treated as a direct sensor of subsurface groundwater storage.

## Rapid proof-of-need

The proposal-stage pilot uses three contrasting hydro-agricultural regimes:

- **Karnal, Haryana:** groundwater-buffered agriculture;
- **Yavatmal, Maharashtra:** rainfall-dependent agriculture;
- **Mettur–Cauvery, Tamil Nadu:** surface-water-buffered agriculture.

The purpose of the pilot is to demonstrate that source-specific water variability differs across regimes and that agricultural water security therefore requires integration of **water source × crop timing × agro-ecological context**.

The pilot does not claim national representativeness, causal attribution, a common stress scale across water sources or complete temporal coverage at every site.
