# Reviewer quick-start

## 60-second scientific case

**Problem:** Agricultural water security cannot be diagnosed from rainfall alone because farming systems differ in how climate variability is stored, accessed and buffered through rainfall, surface water and groundwater.

**Evidence:** A rapid proof-of-need uses three contrasting hydro-agricultural regimes—Karnal (groundwater-buffered), Yavatmal (rainfall-dependent) and Mettur–Cauvery (surface-water-buffered)—to show distinct source-specific temporal behaviour during the 2021–2025 observation window, subject to data availability.

**Gap:** Existing hydrological observations are commonly analysed source by source, whereas farmers experience water security as a crop-stage question: is accessible water available when it is needed?

**Solution:** JAL-AESR INDIA integrates **water source × crop timing × agro-ecological context** within a national NISAR–IRS-enabled framework.

## What the preliminary pilot demonstrates

- water-source regimes behave differently through time;
- agricultural exposure cannot be inferred from annual rainfall alone;
- missingness and source-specific measurement conventions must be handled explicitly;
- a national framework needs observed groundwater, surface-water dynamics, rainfall, crop timing and irrigation dependence in the same analytical architecture.

## What the preliminary pilot does not claim

- national representativeness;
- causal attribution;
- complete temporal coverage at all sites;
- a common physical stress scale across groundwater, rainfall and surface water;
- direct groundwater measurement by NISAR;
- a validated national water-security index.
