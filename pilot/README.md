# Rapid proof-of-need pilot

The pilot is intentionally small. Its purpose is to **demonstrate the need for the proposed national framework**, not to replicate the full JAL-AESR INDIA methodology before the project begins.

## Three hydro-agricultural archetypes

| Pilot | Landscape | Archetype | Preliminary evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Karnal, Haryana | Groundwater-buffered | Groundwater-level variability |
| P2 | Yavatmal, Maharashtra | Rainfall-dependent | Reported rainfall variability |
| P3 | Mettur–Cauvery, Tamil Nadu | Surface-water-buffered | Surface-water-level variability |

## Scientific interpretation

The same climate anomaly may create different agricultural consequences because water can reach crops through different pathways:

**Groundwater-buffered**  
Climate variability → aquifer storage/access → pumping → crop-stage water availability

**Rainfall-dependent**  
Climate variability → rainfall timing/dry spells → crop-stage water availability

**Surface-water-buffered**  
Climate variability → stored/routed surface water → irrigation supply → crop-stage water availability

## Key proposal message

> Agricultural water security is determined not only by how much rain falls, but by where water is stored, how farmers can access it, and whether it is available when crops actually require it.

## Observation window and limitations

The pilot uses available observations within the **2021–2025 analysis window**. Coverage differs by source and site. Missing periods remain explicit.

The pilot does not claim national representativeness or causal attribution. Source-specific standardized anomalies are exploratory visual summaries and must not be interpreted as a universal stress scale.
